Optimizer & model caveats (thesis validity notes)

Purpose
 - Living list of assumptions/limitations that may affect validity, interpretation, and “business realism”.
 - Use this as a checklist for: (1) thesis limitations section, (2) what to justify, (3) what to ask domain experts.


1) Reposition “teleportation” risk (port-coordinate coverage)
 - The optimiser approximates reposition time using port IDs + `ports_latlon.csv` (haversine nautical miles, default speed).
 - Current implementation uses a configurable conservative fallback when the pairwise distance is unavailable:
   `missing_reposition_days` (default 1.0 day in thesis demo runner).
 - Historical note: older runs used 0.0 fallback in some cases, which could allow unrealistic instantaneous moves.
 - Consequence: with conservative fallback, unknown-port arcs can become much harder to schedule (especially if many pairs
   are missing), reducing served voyages but improving realism.
 - Mitigation options (future work):
   - Improve port mapping coverage/ID harmonisation; optionally fail closed for missing ports (drop arcs).
   - Add QA report: % of arcs using real distances vs fallback, and list missing port IDs.
   - Calibrate `missing_reposition_days` with domain feedback (e.g., typical minimum ballast transit time).
 - Current safeguard:
   - `Models/run_thesis_optimizer_demo.py` now includes a DEST coverage preflight and optional fail-fast gate:
     `--require-dest-port-coverage --min-dest-port-coverage 0.90`.


2) Location granularity (port-level only)
 - Vessel “current location” is represented only as an open port (`VESSEL_OPEN_PORT_ID`), not continuous AIS position.
 - Reposition is port-to-port only; there is no modelling of mid-voyage positions, routing constraints, or weather.


3) Waiting costs are implicit (not priced explicitly)
 - The solver can delay starts within time windows via `t_j` choice; waiting does not incur an explicit objective penalty
   unless introduced via additional terms.
 - Consequence: schedules may include long idle times if not discouraged by other objective components.


4) Laycan semantics are simplified / ambiguous
 - Laycan end can be “hard” or “soft with lateness penalty”; the correct real-world interpretation depends on contract/ops.
 - Current evaluation counts `start > laycan_end` as a violation even when lateness is allowed (coverage run).


5) Missing operational constraints (thesis scope)
 - The thesis optimiser does not model many real constraints, e.g.:
   - draft/LOA/beam port restrictions, terminal compatibility
   - capacity/cargo compatibility and sequencing constraints
   - maintenance windows, crew changes, bunkering, canal constraints for reposition legs
 - Consequence: plans are “physically consistent” in time but not necessarily operationally feasible.


6) Economic objective is partial
 - Arc cost uses available voyage-level revenue/cost fields; it is not a full P&L model.
 - If any of the cost/revenue columns are missing or unreliable, the economic ranking of solutions may be distorted.


7) Model prediction uncertainty not represented
 - Sailing time and turnaround predictors are point estimates; optimiser treats them as deterministic durations.
 - Consequence: schedules can be brittle under forecast error; no buffers/robust optimisation currently included.
